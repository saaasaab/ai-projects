W ↑ Drive forward

Accelerate ahead. If reversing, brake first, then move forward.

S ↓ Reverse

Back up slowly. If moving forward, brake first, then reverse.

G Path guide

Toggle a short prediction of the trailer rear’s path at the current steering angle.

Start the turn: while reversing, steer the front wheels opposite the direction you want the trailer’s rear to move, relative to the cab.

Catch it early: once the trailer is turning, steer back to follow it. Straight front wheels do not straighten a bent trailer in reverse.

Reset your angle: if the bend gets too big, stop and pull forward with straight wheels. There is no penalty for taking a pull-up.

Finish the job: back the whole trailer into the green bay, match the arrow’s orientation, and stop. The truck stays outside the bay.

Low-speed articulated vehicle model: a 4.0 m tractor wheelbase, 8.2 m kingpin-to-trailer-axle distance, and a rigid 12 m trailer. Fixed 120 Hz integration, finite steering speed, acceleration, braking, and oriented-body collision checks. Reverse instability comes from the wheel constraints. This models yard maneuvering; it does not simulate tire slip, suspension, or high-speed highway sway. Model background: MathWorks tractor–trailer kinematics.
